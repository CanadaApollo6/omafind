# Project 2 - Does light color change how fast beans grow?
Name: Maya (grade 5), Ms. Okonkwo's class, science fair Feb 28

Question: do bean seedlings grow taller under different colored light?

Hypothesis: blue light makes the plants shorter and stouter. Red light makes them tallest because plants use red for photosynthesis. (Not sure about the red one, we'll see.)

Materials:
- 20 paper cups (9 oz), poked holes in the bottom
- 16 lima bean seeds
- potting soil
- 4 LED grow lights: red, blue, green, white. Got them at the hardware store on Elm, $12 each
- ruler, notebook, tape

Procedure:
1. Planted 4 seeds per cup, 1 inch deep. Watered each cup with 30 mL every 2 days.
2. Put each light 6 inches above one set of 4 cups. On 12 hours a day with a timer.
3. Measured height from soil to top leaf every 3 days for 3 weeks.
4. Kept a fifth set of 4 cups in the window as a control.

Results (average height in cm after 21 days):
- red: 14.2
- blue: 9.6
- green: 4.1
- white: 12.8
- window control: 11.5

Conclusion: blue made the plants shorter, like I thought. Green was the worst, almost no growth. I was wrong that red was best; white was almost as good. Maybe red was too strong and the leaves went pale on 2 cups. Need to check that.

Problems: one red cup tipped over on day 9 when the cord got pulled. We kept the data but Ms. O said to write it down.

Next time: more cups, and maybe a longer experiment with more light colors. Ask Dad why the leaves turned pale.
