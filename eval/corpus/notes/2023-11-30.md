# book notes - The Quiet Ledger of Small Habits (Marisol Brandt, 2019)

Read chapters 1-4 on the train this week. Main idea: behavior change works best when you design the environment instead of relying on willpower. Brandt calls it "friction budgeting".

Things I want to try:
- leave running shoes by the bed (her "visible cue" idea). Did it Sunday, went out Monday at 6:10.
- two-minute rule: if a task takes under two minutes, do it now. Works for dishes, not for taxes.
- move the phone charger to the kitchen so it doesn't live in the bedroom. Tried Tuesday, slept better.
- identity framing: instead of "I want to run more" say "I am someone who shows up." Feels cheesy, but she cites a study with 212 participants, so I'll give it a month.

Quotes to keep:
"You do not rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems." (she attributes this to a coach named Dale Pruitt, who I can't verify)
"Missing once is an accident. Missing twice is the start of a new pattern."

Disagreements: she downplays motivation almost completely and I think that's too strong. My piano practice ran on motivation for six months before the routine took over.

Chapter 5 is about social environment. The kids' soccer schedule might be a good place to test it. Ask Liam's coach about the Saturday practice times.

Next book on my list: Slow Money Rules by Harlan Oyelaran, library copy, due Dec 14. Renew if needed.
