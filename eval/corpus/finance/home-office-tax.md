# home office deduction - notes

Situation: I work from the spare bedroom, the one we call the office. About 11 x 12 ft, call it 132 sq ft. Around 80% of the room is work only. The bed is in the other room. The closet holds the kids' art supplies, which is a mess. The apartment is 1,020 sq ft total, so the room is about 13%.

Two methods:
1. Simplified: $5 per square foot up to 300 sq ft. 132 sq ft gives a $660 deduction. Only needs the measurements, no receipts.
2. Regular (Form 8829): share of rent, utilities, internet and renters insurance. Rent $31,800 a year x 13% is about 4,134. Utilities about 3,400 x 13% is 442. Internet 960 x 13% is 125. Renters insurance 180 x 13% is 23. Total about 4,724.

Problems:
- the regular method needs exclusive use. The art supply closet doesn't count, and the desk gets used for the kids' homework sometimes. That breaks the rule.
- I'm a W-2 employee. Unreimbursed employee expenses are not deductible on the federal return under current law, so my own home office doesn't help.
- Sofia's Harmon contract is self-employment, so any home office deduction goes on her Schedule C, not on our joint return. She mostly works at the kitchen table, not the spare room.

Conclusion for now: if Sofia uses the spare room only for the freelance work, she could use the simplified method on her Schedule C. Otherwise skip it. Ask Marjorie whether the state return allows the same thing.
